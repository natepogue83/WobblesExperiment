**The gene-only regime is useful as an end-to-end positive-pressure check. It is not an analytically known positive reproductive S.**

The mortality rule really is independent of place and current behavior: at eligible dawns it applies `0.02 × (1 − caution / 1.6)` to every living creature. For caution 0.2 versus 0.8, those daily hazards are 0.0175 versus 0.01. A controlled draw of 0.012 killed the low-caution creature and spared the high-caution creature. Its protection did not need to enter that decision. The absence of a prowler also supports omitting gene-off and direct-off after the demonstrated development equality.


However, the measured endpoint is lifetime offspring weighting in the full ecology. Energy, mates, population caps, inherited background traits and replacement births still intervene between survival and offspring. Changing death rates also changes which later creatures enter the birth cohort. The hazard's direction is known; the exact full-ecology reproductive contrast is not. V3 is much better than the old direct-exposure “calibration,” but “nothing behavioural in the way” is still too strong for this endpoint.

The registered rule, a fixed 95% interval for gene-on minus none-on wholly above zero, is defensible as a conservative empirical gate. Keep it fixed. Passing means this chosen regime produced a resolvable positive reproductive signal. Failing means that gate failed; it does not identify a software error, establish that the primary analysis is blind, or authorize retuning and retrying. Add a deterministic gene-hazard regression test; retain the synthetic known-answer S and interaction tests as the actual arithmetic checks. A controlled survival-to-offspring fixture with known truth would further improve validation, but I would not require redesigning the ecological gene arm before October 18.

The development lower bound is genuinely positive under the final 10,000-resample procedure: **0.0003986**, not exactly zero. Under the 2,000-resample precision calculation it was **−0.0001649**. That is a near-boundary Monte Carlo difference, not contradictory underlying simulations. Print sufficient precision and use the frozen procedure once. One successful pilot is not a measured detection limit or coverage study. Evaluation of statistical procedures calls for explicit estimands, data-generating mechanisms and performance measures, with uncertainty from finite simulation repetitions reported. **Delta = 0.01 is an acceptable declared resolution target, but its current justification overstates what the pilot establishes. N = 256 is reasonable for that target.**

S is an offspring-weighted mean caution minus the unweighted cohort mean. A difference of 0.01 is a difference in those caution units; it is not a one-percent change in mortality, a selection coefficient, or a guaranteed change in descendant mean caution. It is about 5.8% of the stated founder SD of 0.174.

“Two-thirds of the strongest pressure we built” is a choice, not a demonstration that smaller effects cannot matter. The reference effect of 0.0153 is noisy, and it is a different contrast from the interaction. The primary E_off pilot is only 0.0101: an interaction below 0.01 could still represent a large fraction of that effect. Equivalence bounds should represent a stated scientific or practical tolerance, not acquire importance merely from a pilot estimate. [Lakens, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177).

I would retain 0.01 and write: “We chose 0.01 as the absolute interaction size this study is designed to resolve. Smaller interactions remain possible and may matter under other questions or longer-term dynamics.” That is defensible without pretending this world supplied a uniquely correct smallest meaningful effect. Changing the pilot threshold from 0.02 to 0.01 before confirmation, openly recorded, does not by itself invalidate the holdout. It makes equivalence harder, not easier.

Detecting I = 0.005 has about **35% power**. Requiring the entire 95% interval inside ±0.01 when true I = 0 succeeds about **77%** of the time. The corresponding rough N for 80% zero-interaction equivalence is 271. These are planning calculations, not measured operating characteristics of this bootstrap or the entire gated decision table. E_off and validation must also pass for the attenuation headline, and the SD estimate itself comes from only 64 seeds.

There is no compelling reason to expand N under the deadline merely to turn 77% into 80%. Keep N = 256 if an inconclusive outcome is acceptable. Do not call it an 85%-powered full attenuation decision or a guaranteed equivalence study.

But a nonfatal attack removes energy, and breeding requires energy at least 0.7; failed strikes remove still more energy. Flight, shelter choices and altered interactions can change lifetime offspring without a predator killing anyone. I counted 397 and 484 recorded `night.attacked` increments in the saved on/off data respectively. These are the existing post-action, living-creature counters, **not a complete count of all attacks**, but they make clear that fatal events are not the whole exposure history.

The point estimate is near 0.01; the interaction estimate is near zero. More seeds can narrow uncertainty about that narrow contrast. They cannot ensure that substantial positive selection exists for learning to attenuate.

**The decision rules are close, but two phrases still promise the wrong conclusion.**

For equivalence, report that the mean interaction is bounded within ±0.01 at the registered confidence level. Replace the closing promise to say learning “does not detectably attenuate” the predator effect. That returns to a detection/non-detection formulation and obscures the actual bound. It also does not rule out a large proportional attenuation of a small E_off.

For negative I, say **“risk learning shifts the predator-related selection differential upward relative to risk learning off.”** Since I = E_off − E_on, negative I means E_on > E_off. It need not strengthen the absolute predator effect: E_off = −0.04 and E_on = −0.01 give I = −0.03 while reducing a negative effect toward zero. The current universal “strengthens” label is false in that case. Require and report the relevant signs before using stronger biological wording.

The branch ordering also allows a precisely estimated positive interaction entirely below delta to receive an attenuation label. That can be valid: evidence of a positive effect and evidence that it is small can coexist. State the ordering explicitly and report both direction and size; “attenuation” must not silently mean “larger than 0.01.” The 95%-interval equivalence rule is conservative relative to conventional 5% TOST using a 90% interval. Keeping the registered 95% rule is fine; do not relax it after seeing confirmation. Positive and practically small effects can coexist. [Lakens, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177).

Some smaller reporting issues are worth fixing without delaying the primary study: “cohort births” sums offspring credited to cohort parents, so a child of two cohort parents contributes twice; label it accordingly. The mechanism table reports only learning-on arms despite saying “every arm,” and uses a pooled correlation of transformed within-colony ranks rather than an average of colony Spearman coefficients; describe that exact statistic. Show zero-night exclusions and degenerate bootstrap counts in the human-readable report. Remove stale world-code comments still calling the direct regime behavior-free. These do not overturn the primary interaction.
